Data center cooling: is protecting control power enough?

Define the protected circuit before choosing a voltage sag countermeasure

2026-10-31
Data center coolingControl powerVoltage sagEquipment assessment
한국어

For data center cooling, assessing voltage sag protection requires more than a protection device’s performance figures. Keeping a control circuit powered and establishing that the complete cooling system will continue operating are separate questions. Identify the supply paths and operating conditions before selecting a device.

Data center cooling: define the function being protected

Consider a hypothetical assessment, not an actual customer case. A facility is evaluating protection for a cooling unit’s control supply. The engineer has information about the proposed protection device, but has not yet checked the compressor power circuit or the drive’s operating conditions.

The assessment can describe the proposed control-power protection. It cannot yet establish that the cooling unit will keep running through a voltage sag. There is no evidence that equipment outside the assessed circuit receives the same protection.

Start with a specific question: what function is this measure intended to preserve? Establishing that scope comes before selecting capacity.

Separate supply, operating command and restart conditions

Three questions help organize the assessment:

Question Information to examine
Which circuit will receive the protected supply? Power and control drawings, and the protection device’s connection scope
Is an operating command still being delivered? Control-circuit descriptions and available operating-state records
Under what conditions will equipment operate again after voltage recovery? Manufacturer restart instructions, protection requirements and interlocks

This is a consultation checklist, not a procedure for declaring equipment healthy or changing its settings.

Schneider Electric’s control-circuit explanation illustrates why supply recovery and restart must be considered separately. Its three-wire arrangement requires a new start signal after a supply interruption. In its two-wire arrangement, equipment restarts when power returns if the operating signal remains present. Actual equipment may include additional control logic: these examples do not establish how every cooling installation behaves. Manufacturer explanation

Voltage tolerance also has conditions

When assessing a contactor, identify the model and whether its coil uses AC or DC. The manufacturer’s TeSys D guidance distinguishes dropout-voltage ranges by product group, coil type and temperature. A single number should not be treated as a universal threshold for all contactors. TeSys D manufacturer guidance

A recorded voltage reduction needs to be compared with the relevant equipment specification. If the model or measurement location is unknown, leave those items open rather than concluding that voltage sag caused the stop.

Prepare the information needed for a consultation

For an application discussion with WESCO, the following information helps define the protection target:

  • Equipment and major control-component model numbers and nameplate details.
  • Drawings showing the power and control supply paths.
  • Manufacturer input specifications and restart instructions.
  • For a reported stop, its time and the availability of equipment alarms and voltage records.

If some records are unavailable, separate confirmed information from unresolved questions. Do not fill those gaps with assumptions. Bypassing protection functions or interlocks to keep equipment running is not a proposed countermeasure here.

Key takeaways

  • Describe control-power protection in terms of its actual connections and conditions.
  • Assess other circuits and equipment requirements before extending that conclusion to compressors, drives or the complete cooling system.
  • Agree on the function to preserve, the circuits already checked and the conditions still unresolved. That makes the scope of a proposed protection measure clear without overstating its effect.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. Does control-power protection establish that the entire cooling system will keep running?
No. Assess the actual protected circuit and separately check any motor, compressor or drive power circuits and operating conditions outside it.
Q. Will equipment restart when voltage returns?
It depends on the control arrangement and equipment requirements. Check the manufacturer's restart instructions and actual control logic rather than assuming automatic restart.
Q. What information helps an application assessment?
Provide equipment model and nameplate details, power and control circuit drawings, manufacturer specifications and any available stop-time, alarm and voltage records.
