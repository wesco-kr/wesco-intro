Single- and three-phase TSP — product ranges and protection scope

Product capacity ranges and individual, group or central compensation describe different things

2026-11-20

Single- and three-phase product labels should be read separately from individual, group and central compensation arrangements. This article distinguishes WESCO TSP product ranges from the installation point and the circuits intended for protection. It does not determine wiring compatibility or select a model for a particular machine.

What the single- and three-phase range figures tell you

WESCO product information lists the small and medium range as single-phase up to 20kVA and three-phase up to 200kVA, with a large-capacity range of 300–2,400kVA. TSP is intended for low-voltage applications at 1kV or below.

These are product-range descriptions. A label such as PLC, sensor or motor is not used here to assign a supply arrangement or a TSP model. For an application assessment, gather the target circuit’s supply specifications alongside those of the proposed model. Without the machine’s circuit diagram and model-specific wiring documentation, this article does not propose a connection method.

Individual, group and central compensation describe installation points

In WESCO installation guidance, individual compensation is placed immediately before a machine’s supply input to serve that machine. Group compensation is installed at a distribution panel to serve the equipment connected downstream. Central compensation is installed downstream of the main ACB in the low-voltage main switchboard.

When preparing an assessment, mark the proposed installation point and the circuits connected downstream, rather than listing only a product name. Keep the term group compensation for the distribution-panel arrangement. Do not select one of these three installation arrangements solely from a single- or three-phase label.

Keep control-circuit protection distinct from whole-machine operation

Control-power protection concerns the connected control circuit. It does not establish that unprotected motor or drive power circuits, or the complete machine, will continue operating through a voltage sag. Assessing actual operation requires the selected model’s specifications, wiring and operating conditions.

For the assessment, mark both the intended protection point and the circuits outside that scope on the circuit diagram. State whether the proposal concerns a control circuit or equipment downstream of a distribution panel. No cost savings or uninterrupted-machine performance are estimated here.

Key takeaways

  • Distinguish single- and three-phase product capacity ranges from selecting a model for an actual application.
  • Use WESCO’s installation points and target scope to distinguish individual, group and central compensation.
  • Do not turn a range description into a wiring-compatibility decision or a guarantee for unprotected circuits.
  • An actual application assessment needs model specifications, the machine’s circuit diagram and its operating conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. How does WESCO describe its single- and three-phase product ranges?
The small and medium range is listed as single-phase up to 20kVA and three-phase up to 200kVA. The large-capacity range is 300–2,400kVA. These range descriptions do not establish wiring compatibility or select a model for a particular machine.
Q. Are group and central compensation installed in the same place?
WESCO describes group compensation at a distribution panel, while central compensation is installed downstream of the main ACB in the low-voltage main switchboard. The actual protection scope requires checking the connected circuits.
Q. Does control-power protection guarantee that the whole machine keeps running?
No such guarantee follows. Control-power protection concerns the connected control circuit; it does not establish ride-through of unprotected drive power circuits or of the complete machine.
